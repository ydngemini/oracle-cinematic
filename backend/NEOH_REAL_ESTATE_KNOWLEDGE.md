# NEOH Real Estate Knowledge Base

## Core Wholesale Concepts

### MAO (Maximum Allowable Offer)
The MAO formula is the hard mathematical gate: `MAO = (ARV × 0.70) - Rehab`.
- ARV = After Repair Value (what the property will sell for after full renovation)
- Rehab = Total renovation cost including materials, labor, permits, and contingency (typically 10-15% added)
- If the seller's asking price exceeds MAO, the deal is automatically flagged for deeper review.
- The 70% multiplier accounts for profit margin, holding costs, closing costs, and risk buffer.

### ARV Estimation
- Based on comparable sales (comps) within 0.5-1 mile radius, sold within last 6-12 months.
- Adjust for square footage differences, condition, age, lot size, and amenities.
- County-level per-sqft baselines: New Castle $195, Kent $155, Sussex $210 (DE reference).
- Always cross-reference with current active listings to gauge market direction.
- Apply upward/downward adjustments for market trends (upward means comps are underpriced, downward means comps are stale).

### Rehab Estimation
- Categorize by year-built bracket: pre-1960 (full gut), 1960-1990 (major systems), 1990-2010 (cosmetic + mechanical), 2010+ (light cosmetic).
- Per-sqft rehab ranges: $15-25 for light cosmetic, $25-50 for mechanical, $50-100+ for full gut.
- Always add 15% contingency to raw rehab estimate.
- Factor in local labor rates, permit fees, and material availability.
- Foundation, roof, HVAC, electrical panel, and plumbing stack are the "big five" — each can add $5-20k.

## Distress Signals (Motivation Indicators)

### High-Yield Signals
- Tax delinquency (unpaid property taxes > 1 year)
- Absentee owner (mailing address differs from property address)
- Probate/estate filings
- Pre-foreclosure / Lis Pendens filings
- Code violations (boarded windows, tall grass, structural citations)
- Water shutoff / utility lien
- Corporate-owned / LLC-owned (easier corporate decision-making)

### Medium-Yield Signals
- High equity (>60% LTV) — owner has room to negotiate
- Long ownership tenure (>15 years) — likely significant equity, low cost basis
- Vacant property (USPS vacant indicator)
- Multiple properties owned by same entity (portfolio sellers)
- Recent divorce, bankruptcy, or judgment filings
- Deed-in-lieu or short sale history

### Low-Yield Signals
- Recent purchase (<2 years) — insufficient equity
- Owner-occupied with homestead exemption — emotional attachment
- Reverse mortgage — complex lien structure
- Active listing with agent — already marketed

## Disposition & Exit Strategies

### Assignment (Wholesale)
- Contract is assigned to end buyer for a fee.
- Assignment fee is the difference between contracted price and end-buyer price.
- Requires an assignable contract (check state laws — some require disclosures).
- Double-close (simultaneous close) when assignment is restricted.
- Must disclose equitable interest and the fact that seller is dealing with a wholesaler.

### Wholetail (Light Rehab + MLS)
- Purchase, minor cosmetic rehab, list on MLS at retail price.
- Typical rehab: paint, flooring, fixtures, landscaping.
- Target 10-20% ROI after rehab and holding costs.

### Buy-and-Hold (Rental)
- Purchase, rehab, tenant-place, refinance (BRRRR method).
- Target 1% rule: monthly rent ≥ 1% of all-in cost.
- Cash-on-cash return target: 8-12%.
- Factor property management (8-10% of rent), vacancy (5-8%), maintenance (10-15%).

## Risk & Compliance

### Fair Housing
- Never discriminate based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability.
- Marketing materials must not imply preference or exclusion.
- Prohibited fields in data analysis: race, ethnicity, religion, national origin, citizenship, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, familial status, pregnancy, disability, medical condition, veteran status, age of occupant, children, credit score, consumer credit data.

### Public Record Constraints
- All intelligence must cite observable, verifiable public records.
- No covert profiling or inferred personal characteristics.
- Zoning, title, and lien conclusions require professional review.
- Legal documents and redlines must be reviewed by licensed attorney.
- Tax estimates are informational, not guaranteed.

### State-Specific Notes
- DE (Delaware): Assignable contracts with disclosure; 3-day right of rescission
- PA (Pennsylvania): Requires written disclosure of equitable interest
- NJ (New Jersey): Strict disclosure requirements; attorney review period
- MD (Maryland): Requires real estate license for more than 3 wholesale deals/year

## Market Analysis

### Key Metrics
- Median DOM (Days on Market) — rising DOM signals cooling market
- Absorption rate — months of inventory; <3 = seller's market, >6 = buyer's market
- List-to-sale price ratio — gap widening signals negotiation room
- Inventory levels — absolute count and month-over-month trend
- Rent-to-price ratio — <0.5% suggests sell, >0.8% suggests hold
