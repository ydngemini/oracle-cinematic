# Delaware Property Law — Assignment Contract Reference

Source: Delaware Code, Title 25 (Property), Title 6 (Commerce and Trade)

---

## Title 25, Chapter 61 — Conveyances (Key Sections)

### § 2501. Statute of Frauds
No action shall be brought to charge any person upon any contract for the sale
of lands, tenements, or hereditaments, or any interest in or concerning them,
unless the contract or agreement upon which such action shall be brought, or some
memorandum or note thereof, shall be in writing and signed by the party to be
charged therewith, or by some other person thereunto by him or her lawfully
authorized.

### § 2502. Deed Requirements
Every deed or conveyance of lands, tenements, or hereditaments shall be signed
and sealed by the grantor and attested by at least one credible witness, and
acknowledged before a judge or notary public.

### § 2513. Recording Requirements
All deeds and conveyances shall be recorded in the office of the Recorder of
Deeds in the county where the lands lie. Unrecorded instruments are void as
against subsequent bona fide purchasers.

## Title 6, Chapter 25 — Assignment of Rights

### § 2-210. Delegation of Duties; Assignment of Rights (UCC)
(1) A party may perform his duty through a delegate unless otherwise agreed or
unless the other party has a substantial interest in having his original promisor
perform or control the acts required by the contract.
(2) Unless otherwise agreed, all rights of either seller or buyer can be assigned
except where the assignment would materially change the duty of the other party,
or increase materially the burden or risk imposed on him by his contract.

## Delaware Real Estate Commission — Key Regulations

### Wholesaling Disclosure Requirements
- Delaware does NOT currently require a real estate license for wholesale
  assignment of contracts, provided the assignor never takes title.
- The assignor must have an equitable interest (executed purchase contract)
  before marketing the assignment.
- Marketing a property for which one does not hold a contract constitutes
  unlicensed brokerage activity.

### Anti-Fraud Provisions
- Assignment fees must be disclosed to all parties.
- Double-closing (simultaneous close) is permitted but requires separate
  HUD-1/ALTA settlement statements.
- Misrepresentation of property condition or value constitutes fraud under
  Delaware Code Title 6, § 2513.

## Red Flags for Legal Sentinel

The following conditions should trigger a LEGAL HOLD regardless of MAO math:

1. **No assignability clause** — contract is silent on assignment rights
2. **Anti-assignment language** — "This contract may not be assigned"
3. **Seller consent required** — creates execution risk
4. **Expired notary commission** — document may be challenged
5. **Missing recording reference** — instrument not recorded
6. **Probate/estate issues** — seller is estate without letters testamentary
7. **Contract expired** — closing deadline has passed
8. **Title contingency absent** — no clear title guarantee
9. **Earnest money held by assignor** — conflict of interest, not in escrow
10. **Cross-county recording** — instrument recorded in wrong county
